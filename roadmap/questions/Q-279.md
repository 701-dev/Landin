# Q-279 — How does a release get its FreeBSD arm64 archive?

Asked by R21.61 on 2026-11-25.

R21.61 builds `refine` on FreeBSD arm64 and makes
`landin-VERSION-aarch64-freebsd.tar.gz` with `compiler/tests/freebsd/host.py
--mode=release --package`, in the FreeBSD arm64 guest under KVM on your
arm64 host. The release workflow cannot make it the way it makes the
x86-64 one: GitHub's arm64 runners have no KVM, and building the pinned
GCC in an emulated guest outlasts a job's six hours. Your rule keeps CI
off your machines. What reaches users, and what the release attests, is
yours to decide.

**Options:**

1. The archive is made by a named run in the arm64 guest at each release
   and attached to the release by hand (`gh release upload`), with its
   `.sha256`; the release workflow does not attest it, and the release
   notes say so.
2. The toolchain archive the guest builds, 111 MB and named by its inputs'
   digest, is published once as a pinned asset; the release workflow
   fetches it by digest and builds only `refine` in an emulated guest,
   which fits a job, and attests the archive as it does the others.
3. No FreeBSD arm64 archive is published; FreeBSD arm64 users build
   from source with `environments/freebsd/gnat.py` and `host.py`.

**Recommendation:** option 2 once the emulated `refine` build is measured
to fit a job; option 1 until then.

**Default until answered:** the named run makes the archive and its
checksum, kept with the item's evidence; no release publishes it, the
release workflow is unchanged, and `docs/capabilities.md` says the
archive is made by the host lane but not yet published.

**What waits on the answer:** the first release that would publish a
FreeBSD arm64 archive; nothing in R21.61.
