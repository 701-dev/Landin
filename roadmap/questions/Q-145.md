# Q-145 — Should `core/failure` stop importing the modules whose atoms it names?

Asked by R20.10 on 2026-11-22.

The inventory's third question. `core/failure` imports eight modules only
to name their atoms in `failure.reason` and give each its words (D388).
`core/fibre`, `hosted/thread` and `platform/posix` import it, so importing
`core/fibre` pulls in nearly the whole of `core`. D296 drops unreachable
bindings from an image, so the cost may be compile work rather than
flash; nobody has measured it.

**Options:**

1. Keep the one registry; R20.40 measures what importing `core/fibre`
   costs a Cortex-M image and splits only if the measurement shows a
   cost.
2. Split now: each module keeps its atoms' words beside its atoms, and
   `core/failure` becomes a small concept the three importers use without
   the registry.
3. Invert: `core/fibre`, `hosted/thread` and `platform/posix` stop
   importing `core/failure`, reporting in their own atom sets; the
   registry stays for programs and examples that want one place.

**Recommendation:** option 1. The finding is an edge in the graph, not a
measured cost, and D296 may already make it free in an image.

**Default until answered:** keep the one registry and decide on R20.40's
firmware measurement.

**What waits on the answer:** R20.40's split or keep.

**Answer (2026-11-22, the maintainer):** option 1. Recorded as D1950.
