# Q-274 — Is the growing arena `core/zone`, a module of its own?

Asked by R16.38 on 2026-11-25.

R16.38 adds the library's first allocator that both grows and
bump-allocates. It takes blocks from a parent, each twice the size of
the one before, and hands out each block's bytes in order. `reset`
gives every allocation back through the handle and keeps the newest
block. `release` gives every block back to the parent. binary-trees
moved onto it executes 55% fewer instructions. A new public module is
part of the library's shape, so the name is yours. `core/arena` is the
fixed allocator over caller bytes, and its `arena.release(arena)` is
already a reset. The library has no overloading and keeps one name per
idea (D2028), so a growing form inside `core/arena` would need names of
its own for `over`, `reset` and `release`. The item wrote the
allocator as `core/zone`: `zone.space`, `zone.over(space, parent)`,
`zone.zone(provider)`, `zone.reset(handle)` and `zone.release(space,
parent)`, the same shape as `core/region`.

**Options:**

1. `core/zone`, a module of its own, as written.
2. A growing form inside `core/arena`, `arena.growing(provider)` made by
   `arena.over_parent`, with names of its own for the reset and the
   release.
3. Another module name you choose, such as `core/bump` or `core/chunk`,
   with the same calls.

**Recommendation:** option 1. The fixed arena and the growing one have
different state, a different parent and a different release. A module
of its own keeps every call's short name, and its shape is
`core/region`'s, the other provider over a parent.

**Default until answered:** the growing arena is the module `core/zone`,
with `space`, `over`, `zone`, `reset` and `release`, as R16.38 wrote it.

**What waits on the answer:** nothing in R16.38. A different answer
renames the module, through the breaking list if that comes after a
release.
