# Q-337 — May a library module keep its failures' words, as `core/regex` now does?

Asked by R22.60 on 2026-11-26.

D388 puts every library failure atom's words in `core/failure`'s
`failure.reason`, and D1950, your choice of 2026-11-22, keeps that one
registry until a measurement shows its cost. `core/regex`'s four atoms
would make `core/failure` import `core/regex`, and through its default
options `core/unicode`'s tables. Every hosted program reaches
`core/failure` through `hosted/system`: a program importing only
`core/failure` checks in 0.8 seconds and one that also imports
`core/regex` in 3.0, so the registry would add about two seconds to every
hosted program's check, every hosted fixture's run in the gate among them.
Images do not change either way. R22.60 has `core/regex` keep its own
`regex.words` (D2683) and `check.py` excuse the atoms a module's own
`words` names. The registry's shape is yours.

**Options:**

1. A module may keep its atoms' words, as D2683 has it, where
   `core/failure` importing it would cost the programs that import
   `core/failure`; the rest stay in `failure.reason`.
2. One registry: `core/failure` imports `core/regex`, and the check time
   is paid, as D1950 accepted for the modules it imports now.
3. Split the registry everywhere: each module keeps its own words, and
   `failure.reason` goes.

**Recommendation:** option 1: it keeps D1950's registry for the modules
that cost nothing and leaves one rule, which the check enforces, for the
ones that would.

**Default until answered:** `core/regex` keeps its own words, `regex.words`,
and `check.py` excuses an atom a module's own `words` names.

**What waits on the answer:** nothing in R22.60, which closes on the
default.

**Answer (2026-11-27, the maintainer):** option 1: a module may keep its atoms' words where `core/failure` importing it would cost the programs that import `core/failure`, and the rest stay in `failure.reason`; D2683 is the rule, and `check.py`'s exception for an atom its module's own `words` names stands.
