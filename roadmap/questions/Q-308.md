# Q-308 — Do `f32` and `f64` get `cmp.ordered` evidence?

Asked by R22.10 on 2026-11-25.

R22.10 was to take from R20 whether floats get ordering evidence, and R20
closed without deciding it. `cmp.ordered`'s `less` must be a strict weak
order, and IEEE's `<` is not one once NaN is among the values: NaN is
neither less nor greater than anything, so a sort's result depends on
where the NaNs fell. IEEE 754's totalOrder is a strict order of every bit
pattern: negative NaNs, negative infinity, the negative numbers, -0.0,
+0.0, the positive numbers, infinity, positive NaNs. [1280] gives a type
one evidence for a concept and no override, so whatever `core` registers
for `f64` is the only order a program can sort `f64` by; another order
goes on a distinct type, as for an integer.

**Options:**

1. No evidence: a program sorts floats through a distinct type of its own
   with the order it wants.
2. `core/cmp` gives `f32` and `f64` evidence by totalOrder, so a sort
   works on floats as it does on integers, -0.0 before +0.0 and NaNs at
   the ends.
3. Evidence by `<`, documented as valid only where no NaN is sorted.

**Recommendation:** option 2: it is a strict order, it agrees with `<` on
every number except the two zeros, and it is what a sort of measurements
wants.

**Default until answered:** option 1, no evidence, since option 2 is easy
to add and hard to take back.

**What waits on the answer:** nothing in R22.10; R22.74's distributions
and any sort of floats in a later item.
