`core/dense`'s `f64` product at n=1000 takes 0.41 s on one Zen 2 core, 8.2 times OpenBLAS's 0.049 s (D2703). Most of the difference is vector instructions: refine emits scalar SSE2 only, two flops an instruction where OpenBLAS's kernel does eight with AVX2's fused multiply-add, at any `--level`. The rest is the kernel's own loop at `--optimize speed`: in `products.ldn`'s 4 by 2 `kernel` every element loaded from the packed operands is also stored to its frame slot though nothing reads it there, and a product is written to a frame temporary and added from it rather than from its register, so each round of eight multiply-adds carries about twelve stores and loads it needs none of. A 4 by 4 kernel, sixteen sums against x86-64's sixteen float registers, spilled half its sums and was slower, 3.6 GFLOP/s against 4.9. Met in R22.15.

Completion: the kernel's round keeps its loads and products in registers, measured with `scripts/dense_timing.py`; vector instructions are their own item.
