On the STM32H753 a shorter routine can make a later loop slower, by where the code lands rather than by what it executes. R16.29's boards run (45088038656) made 330 of 342 changed configurations faster, the STM32H753 0.896 at the geometric mean, and 12 slower, all on that board: the sieve at speed 10,646 cycles against 9,295 (+14.5%), binary-search at speed +2.9%, and at `none` the sieve +5.8% and fannkuch-redux +4.6%. At speed the whole sieve image differs from the base only at one routine's entry, `ldr r3, [sp, #0]` in place of `mov r6, sp; ldr r0, [r6]; mov r3, r0`, once a call, which moves every later instruction 4 bytes; the loops are the same instructions. The M7 fetches 64 bits at a time from its instruction memory, and a loop head's place in that fetch decides its cost; R16.27 found the same board times a load by its distance from the store before it (SR-1430).

Completion: Loop heads at the Thumb-2 speed levels aligned where the M7 fetches them best, or the cause measured otherwise, with the STM32H753's sieve and binary-search at speed no slower than their R16.27 figures.
