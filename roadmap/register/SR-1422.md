x86-64's allocator offers nine general registers, rbx, r12-r15, rsi, rdi, r8 and r9; rax, rcx, rdx, r10 and r11 are selection's scratch everywhere, though C's code uses fifteen. A loop with more live values keeps the rest in the frame: SHA-256's compression round keeps three of its eight words there and reloads the round count's bound, and SipHash-1-3's byte loop reloads its slice's pointer and length from the frame for every byte. A SHA-256 of 64 MiB (refine-build's `hash/sha256`, the same digest as gcc's) at speed-auto takes 0.64 s and 8.90 G instructions against gcc 16.1 `-O2 -march=x86-64 -mno-sha`'s 0.35 s and 4.56 G, R16.24's rotate (D1624) included, on an EPYC 7302.

Kept: R19's breaking window changes the language and no backend, allocator or inliner, so nothing since it was filed has measured or touched what it records.

Completion: The scratch registers offered where selection does not write them, as `Touches` does for rsi to r9, with the SHA-256 measured nearer C's.
