On the RP2350's Cortex-M33 and Hazard3, which fetch from SRAM without a cache, a 32-bit instruction at a halfword address in a hot loop costs a cycle each time round. R16.33's boards run (45237321905) took two instructions out of firmware fannkuch-redux's `fannkuch` ahead of its flip loop; the loop's `ldrb.w` head and its `bl` moved from word to halfword addresses, and at speed it ran 0.64% slower on the M33 and 0.63% on the Hazard3 while at size it ran 1.8% and 2.4% faster. Neither backend aligns a loop head or a 32-bit instruction at speed, as x86-64 aligns its loop heads.

Completion: at speed on Thumb-2 and on RV32 with compressed instructions a loop's head, and where it pays a hot 32-bit instruction, sits at a word address, padded only where the padding is not executed, measured over every board's cycles and size.
