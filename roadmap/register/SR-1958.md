arm64 makes a stored value in its slot's register only when the store is the value's last use (D2164), so a value read again after its store is made in a register of its own and copied: reverse-complement's `write_reversed` copies `at - 1` into `at`, the loaded byte into `base` and `width + 1` into `width`, three `mov`s of its eighteen instructions a byte. R16.42 removed them by hand from the arm64 image at speed: 1.597 G instructions to 1.415 G under QEMU, 11.4% fewer, the same output. x86-64's D2157 already lets later uses read the slot's register, and D2654 makes a value there when the slot's interval opens at the store; arm64 has neither.

Completion: arm64 makes a value stored into a slot held in a register there when it is read after the store, measured on reverse-complement.
