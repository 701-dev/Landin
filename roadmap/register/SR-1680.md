A loop's index is bounded at its head's comparison, but checked arithmetic on it, and the bounds check that arithmetic then indexes, are not proved from that range. After R16.30 SHA-256's round loop is 48 x86-64 instructions an iteration, while its message schedule (`for t in usize(16)..<64`, reading `w[t - 15]`, `w[t - 2]`, `w[t - 16]` and `w[t - 7]`) spends 44, each subtraction checked for underflow and each index checked against 64, and its load loop (`for t in usize(0)..<16`, reading `block[4 * t + k]`) spends 60, each `4 * t` a checked `mulq` and each `+ k` a checked add. Per 64-byte block that is 960 + 2,112 of about 7,400 instructions; gcc 16.1 `-O2` spends about 4,350 on the whole block. SHA-256 at x86-64 speed executes 7.76 G instructions on 64 MiB against gcc's 4.56 G.

Completion: An index's range from its loop, carried through constant offsets and multiples to the checks they feed, with SHA-256's schedule and load loops measured without their underflow, overflow and bounds checks where the range proves them.
