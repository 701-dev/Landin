An increment of an index just checked below a constant bound keeps its overflow check, though the check it passed leaves the index at most the bound less one: reverse-complement's `write_reversed` stores into `line[width]`, `line` being `[61]u8`, then `inc width`, and tests the carry of that addition every byte, `jc` on x86-64 and `adds` with `b.cs` on arm64. With the check removed by hand from R16.42's base x86-64 image at speed, reverse-complement executes 1.428 G instructions against 1.488 G, 4.0% fewer, the same output, and the one instruction a byte is about 4.4% of the 1.359 G R16.42 leaves. Unlike `SR-1918`'s count, the fact needs no relation between two values: the bounds check before it in the same block bounds the index.

Completion: such an increment's check proved and removed on every target, measured.
