`math.sqrt` of an `f64` is a call to `sqrt_f64`, whose `assembler.block` around one `sqrtsd` or `fsqrt` is never copied into its caller: on x86-64 at speed the routine is 17 instructions with its frame and the call another eight, so n-body's `advance`, a square root for each of its ten pairs a step, spends about 250 M of the 1.53 G instructions of 1,000,000 steps on them, some 16%, where gcc's `-O2` takes 0.60 G for the same algorithm; arm64's routine is 22 instructions. D2551 left a builtin open for when a measurement shows the block's cost, and this is that measurement, by the routine's length rather than a patch.

Completion: a square root in a hot loop costs its one instruction on every target with a float unit, as a copied block or a builtin, measured on n-body.
