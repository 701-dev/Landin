x86-64 places a slot in a register over its whole interval without counting the registers the values inside that interval still need, so a loop with more slots than registers spills its round's temporaries or keeps its own bound in the frame. After R16.28's eleven registers (D2088) SHA-256's compression round (refine-build's `hash/sha256`, 64 MiB) still keeps `d` and `h` in the frame and reloads its bound of 64 every round: 8.50 G instructions at speed against gcc 16.1 `-O2`'s 4.56 G. Giving `%r8`/`%r9` to more slots made it 8% worse and capping slots at seven to nine registers cost reverse-complement 2.6% to 6.3%; R16.28's loop layout also moved the round from 59 to 61 instructions through a changed interval.

Completion: Slot placement that weighs the pressure of the values inside an interval, with SHA-256's round holding its eight words and its bound in registers, measured.

R16.30 took the pressure's cost by other means, the bound an immediate (D2148), a value without a register read where its one use lies (D2156) and a stored value made in its slot's register (D2157): the round fell from 58 to 48 instructions and SHA-256 at x86-64 speed to 7.76 G. `h` stays in the frame, where a register would save two memory accesses a round and no instruction, so pressure-weighing placement measured no gain; the maintainer kept the row until it is solved (Q-204).

Kept: R16.35 measured the round at 48 instructions, gcc's own count, and found `h` needs a twelfth register: the round's nine slots and the three temporaries it holds at once against the eleven x86-64 offers with `%rcx` and `%rdx` kept for selection's scratch; giving `%r8` and `%r9` to every u32 slot alone made SHA-256 23% worse (5.63 G to 6.95 G at speed, `h` in a register, the temporaries in the frame), and Q-204's answer keeps the row until it is solved.

Kept: R22.10 made D2157 wait until no other value holds the slot's register, a correctness fix inside the stored-value choice, which placed no slot otherwise and left the gate's measured sizes and instruction counts within their baselines.

Kept: R16.42 lets a value take the register of a slot whose interval opens at the value's store (D2654), and placed no slot otherwise.
