Firmware targets, Cortex-M, RV32 and Xtensa, keep lowering's block order (D2096 runs `Landin.IR.Layout` for hosted targets alone): a loop's test at its head, the body, the code after the loop, then the step, which jumps back to the head. Each iteration takes a jump and a branch where a loop tested at its foot takes one branch, and the head is fallen into on entry, so D2308's word alignment, which pads only where nothing falls through, reaches few firmware loops. On x86-64 the hosted layout took 0.5% to 8.9% off every workload at speed.

Completion: the firmware targets lay a loop out tested at its foot where it pays, measured over the Cortex-M lanes' instructions, every board's cycles and every firmware image's size.
