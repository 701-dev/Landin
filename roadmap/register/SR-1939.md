The Tree-sitter grammar refuses an assignment of a sum with two operators, `r = a + b - c` or `r = a - b - c`, when another statement follows it: it reads `r = a + b` and then a block value `- c`, and the next statement is an error. `r = a - c` and `r = a + (b - c)` parse. R22.30 met it in `core/unicode/normalization.ldn`, where `./highlight/test.sh --integration` failed on `result = first + second - t_base`, and wrote `first + (second - t_base)`; no source on main had the form before. Two causes were tried and are not it: the measurement rule accepting any identifier as its operator, which reads `c` and the next line's `x` as a measurement and is a defect of its own, and the assignment's static precedence of 100; neither `prec.right` on `binaryLevel`'s flat list nor a lower dynamic precedence on a block's value changes the parse.

Completion: the grammar parses a sum of three or more operands followed by any statement, with a corpus test under `highlight/tree-sitter/test/corpus/`, the measurement's operator restricted to the word `lenof`, and the line in `core/unicode/normalization.ldn` written without its parentheses again.
