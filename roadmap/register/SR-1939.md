The Tree-sitter grammar refuses an assignment of a sum with two operators, `r = a + b - c` or `r = a - b - c`, when another statement follows it: it reads `r = a + b` and then a block value `- c`, and the next statement is an error. `r = a - c` and `r = a + (b - c)` parse. R22.30 met it in `core/unicode/normalization.ldn`, where `./highlight/test.sh --integration` failed on `result = first + second - t_base`, and wrote `first + (second - t_base)`; no source on main had the form before. R22.60 met it again in `core/regex/compile.ldn`, `other: usize = count - 1 - step` before `low: u8 = sequence.low[step]`, which the measurement rule read as `step low`, and wrote `count - (1 + step)`. R22.20 met it in `core/text/shortest.ldn`, `e2 = 1 - bias - i32(mantissa_bits) - 2` before `m2 = mantissa`, and wrote `1 - (bias + i32(mantissa_bits) + 2)`; R22.45 met it in `runtime/core-text-parse-radix`, `least == 0 - 127 - 1` inside an assignment before `return`, and wrote `least + 1 == 0 - 127`; restricting the operator to `alias('lenof', $.identifier)` leaves `tree-sitter generate` asking for new conflicts between `indexed_expression`, `declaration_reference` and `of_keyword`. Two causes were tried and are not it: the measurement rule accepting any identifier as its operator, which reads `c` and the next line's `x` as a measurement and is a defect of its own, and the assignment's static precedence of 100; neither `prec.right` on `binaryLevel`'s flat list nor a lower dynamic precedence on a block's value changes the parse.

Completion: the grammar parses a sum of three or more operands followed by any statement, with a corpus test under `highlight/tree-sitter/test/corpus/`, the measurement's operator restricted to the word `lenof`, and the lines in `core/unicode/normalization.ldn`, `core/regex/compile.ldn` and `core/text/shortest.ldn` written without their parentheses again.
