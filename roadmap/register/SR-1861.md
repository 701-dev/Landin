Every call that can fail costs x86-64 about eleven instructions on its succeeding way where two would do: its status is stored to the frame and loaded back, the load is validated against its atom set (`cmpl $0; je; cmpl $22; jne` to a conversion trap), and the failure test makes a byte with `setne`, moves it and compares it again before branching. R16.39 measured two parts on binary-trees 18 at x86-64 speed, 21.05 G instructions: sealing the call's failure slot, which the verifier already holds to the callee's errors, so its loads are not validated, gave 1.9%, and fusing the failure test into the branch after it, as a comparison is, 3.9% together, about 5% of cycles; the other programs moved under 0.6%. Sealing changes every backend's validation, so firmware images and board records move with it.

Completion: a call's succeeding way tests its status once, in a register, on every target, measured.
