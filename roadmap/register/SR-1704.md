After R16.31 SHA-256's loops keep no check their index's range proves, and x86-64 at speed executes 6.90 G instructions on 64 MiB against gcc's 4.56 G. What its two loops still spend is selection, not checks. The schedule (`w[t - 15]`, `w[t - 2]`, `w[t - 16]`, `w[t - 7]`) makes each `t - k` in a register of its own, `movq` then `subq`, where the constant belongs in the address's displacement: 31 instructions an iteration where about 23 would do. The load loop makes `4 * t` four times, each a `movq $4` and an `imulq` rather than one scaled index or `shl`, and checks `4 * t`, `4 * t + 1`, `4 * t + 2` and `4 * t + 3` against `lenof block` one by one, the slice's length being dynamic; arm64 makes the same `movz` and `mul` four times.

Completion: an element index that is a value plus or minus a constant addresses with the constant in the displacement on x86-64 and arm64, an identical proved product in a block is made once, and a product by a power of two is a shift or a scaled index, measured on SHA-256 at speed.
