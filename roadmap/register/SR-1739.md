At size, a routine whose body only calls another and returns what it gives is kept as a call, so a library that puts a system's call behind a per-system helper pays a frame and a call for it. R21.20 split `hosted/heap`'s and `hosted/thread/lock`'s system calls into `posix.ldn` and `windows.ldn` helpers, and every hosted Linux image at size grew by about 200 bytes (binary-trees 55,156 to 55,365 bytes of text): `hosted.thread.lock.wake_one` and `sleep_while` forward to `platform/posix`, `release_pages` and `page_bytes` to their mappings. The baselines declare the growth.

Completion: the size profile copies a forwarding routine into its callers, or the library's per-system helpers are the system calls themselves, and the declared growth is given back.

Kept: R22.24 changed process and measurement tools, not the inliner or `hosted/heap`; the row waits for its R16 item or the library review.

Kept: R21.70 met it again: platform/linux's public level wrappers over the new `platform/x86_64` cost every Linux image above x86-64-v1 about 300 bytes at none and size, and the entry's check now calls `platform/x86_64` itself; the size profile still keeps a forwarding routine as a call.

Kept: aged past ten by items that changed neither the inliner's weights nor `hosted/heap`; the next R16 item on the weights or the library review of `hosted/heap` takes it.
