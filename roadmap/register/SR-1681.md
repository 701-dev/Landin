arm64 (D2164) and x86-64 (D2157) make a value stored into a slot's register in that register; RISC-V and Cortex-M still compute it in a register of its own and move it. R16.30 ported D2164's rule to RISC-V's recorded-writes allocator as it stands, and RV64 at speed executed 7% to 110% more instructions: the value then writes the slot's register inside the slot's interval, the check of the second emission against the plan refuses it, and the routine falls back to its homes (D1503, D2108). On arm64 the rule alone took 5.5% to 14% off the hosted workloads at speed.

Completion: RISC-V, RV64 and RV32, and Cortex-M make such a value in its slot's register with the plan's check accepting it, measured on RV64's instructions and the boards' cycles.
