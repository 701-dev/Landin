binary-trees at depth 21 took 2.73 to 2.86 times C's time on Linux x86-64 at `--optimize speed` with 0.2.9, 0.2.10 and main at 668966f97, measured together with `scripts/benchmarks.py` on 2026-11-20, and 2.39 after R16.24's heap changes (main at 58368d890). `docs/benchmarks.md`'s "Closing the remaining gap" recorded 32.8 seconds, 2.22 times C's, at a4f9138d8 in October. The images of 0.2.10 and 668966f97 were the same bytes, so the earlier change came before 0.2.9: the compiler, the library's `core/region` or `hosted/heap`, or how the host placed the run. Met preparing the release after 0.2.10.

The cause is the port's allocator, not code generation (2026-11-20, depth 18, core 15): Landin executed 48.9 billion instructions against C's 13.9 billion at about the same rate per cycle, with fewer cache misses than C, and 77% of its time was `core/region` and `hosted/heap` (`record_allocation` 19%, `host_alloc` 17%, `region_alloc` 9%, `host_free` 8%, `class_of` 7%, `release` 5%). A region asks the heap for every node, writes a 16-byte ledger record of it and frees each one back at `release`. The same program with each tree in a `core/arena` over one heap block sized for its depth, `arena.over_unchecked` over `mem.allocate`d bytes and `arena.reset` after each tree, with identical output, ran depth 21 in 13.31 seconds against C's 15.19 and today's port's 35.97, peak 197 MB against 264 and 394, and 20.3 billion instructions at depth 18; over the five ports that is 1.08 times C's time rather than 1.32. No allocator in the library both grows and bump-allocates, which is SR-1412.
