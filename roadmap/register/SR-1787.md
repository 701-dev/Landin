SHA-256's round on x86-64 is 48 instructions, gcc 16.1 `-O2`'s count. gcc writes its majority `(a & b) ^ (a & c) ^ (b & c)` as `(a & (b ^ c)) ^ (b & c)`, 6 instructions where Landin spends 8, and the choice `(e & f) ^ (~e & g)` as `(e & (f ^ g)) ^ g` would save 2 more: about 6.8% of SHA-256 of 64 MiB at speed (R16.35's 5.63 G). Both identities need a value the source never computed, `b ^ c` or `f ^ g`, and `Landin.IR.Simplification` only rewrites an instruction in place: the choice's `g` is loaded after every position the rewrite could reuse.

Completion: the simplification able to make an instruction where an identity needs one, the two identities applied on every target, and SHA-256's round measured below gcc's.
